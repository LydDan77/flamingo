# Collection Two: creative brief

Status: draft for approval. Collection One (Dark, Light, Warm, Bright, Elite) is locked at tag `v1.0-collection-one` and is not touched by anything in this brief.

## 1. What Round 2 is for

Round 1 proved the engine: one codebase, one content model, five skins, a partner layer and a compliance baseline. Round 2 is about selling power. Every theme below is designed to make two people act within two seconds of landing:

- **The partner** (chiropractor, med-spa owner, physician adding a regenerative or longevity line) must think "this looks more expensive and more credible than anything my competitors run" before they have read a word. They buy done-for-you credibility, speed to revenue and differentiation.
- **The patient** must see, in one glance, what this is, that it is medical and legitimate, that it comes to them, and what to do next.

Round 1 themes leaned on colour identity. Round 2 themes lean on typography, depth and light: layering, shading and fading do the work that neon and hard shadows did before.

## 2. Buyer psychology, and what it changes on the page

Three patient jobs drive every decision:

| Job | Who | Dominant emotion | What wins them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Avoid or delay surgery, move without the daily ache | 45 to 70, joint and soft-tissue pain | Fear of being sold to, hope for a real alternative | Physician oversight, an honest candidacy answer, "we come to you", financing |
| Perform and recover like a professional | 35 to 55 executives and athletes | Impatience, desire for an edge | Precision, labs, protocols, speed, discretion |
| Age with energy and clarity | 50 to 70, labs drifting, mirror changing | Quiet worry, desire for control | Clinician-led planning, one team, one record, plain language |

Patterns that convert this audience, all compliant:

- **Authority before persuasion.** Credentials, sourcing standards and physician oversight appear above the fold, not in a footer.
- **Honesty as the hook.** "If you are not a candidate, we will tell you" and "what we will not promise" blocks outperform hype with this audience and keep counsel happy.
- **Convenience as the differentiator.** In-home delivery by a nurse practitioner and telehealth for protocols are the two facts competitors cannot copy cheaply. Say them early, everywhere.
- **Low-commitment first step.** "See if you are a candidate" beats "Book now" for the pain audience; keep "Book a free consultation" as the primary action and the candidacy check as the secondary one.
- **Money without flinching.** A visible financing line and "programs from" pricing (client-supplied) remove the silent objection.
- **No false urgency, no cures, no before-and-after medical claims.** Scarcity only if it is true (for example, genuine weekly in-home capacity) and client-confirmed.

## 3. The two-second scan: content rules every theme follows

Each page has a scan layer and a read layer. The scan layer must sell the idea on its own.

**Hero formula (scan layer)**

1. Eyebrow: category and modality (`Regenerative medicine · In-home · Telehealth`).
2. Headline: outcome-framed, claim-safe, eight words or fewer. Examples: "Regenerative care that comes to you", "A non-surgical path your doctor can stand behind", "Longevity medicine, prescribed on your numbers".
3. Subline: for whom and how, one sentence.
4. Three proof chips: `Physician-led`, `NP-delivered at home`, `Every lot tested`.
5. Primary action plus a low-commitment secondary action.
6. One trust line: `Licensed clinicians · Accredited tissue banks · Financing available`.

**Section ladder.** Eyebrow (context) → one bold, claim-safe benefit sentence → one supporting line → proof or visual. Nobody should need the third line to understand the section.

**Proof stack, in this order:** physician oversight → NP in the home → sourcing and lot testing → licensed telehealth → financing → reviews with disclosures.

**Objection cards on every service page:** Is it safe? Is it for me? What does it cost? What happens on the day?

**New content modules to build once and use across all five themes**

- "What we will not promise" (the honesty block)
- "Who this is not for"
- "Your first 30 days" timeline
- "The sourcing standard" (accredited tissue banks, lot testing, documentation on request)
- "Questions to ask any provider" (positions the client as the honest one)
- "Cost and financing" transparency block
- A non-diagnostic candidacy checker that ends in a consultation, never in an answer

**Numbers.** Only verified, client-supplied figures. Placeholders keep their `Client to confirm` tags.

**Compliance guardrails (unchanged from the Collection One pass)**

- "May support", "studied for", "aimed at": never "cures", "heals", "regrows", "guaranteed".
- FDA statement and, where relevant, the California § 24185 notice stay on every therapy page.
- Testimonials carry individual-results and compensation disclosures; no before-and-after medical imagery.
- Prescription items are "prescribed by a clinician based on your labs", never sold.
- Stem cell therapy is never implied to be FDA-approved.
- Clinical review board sign-off on any new medical copy.

## 4. Design principles for the collection

- **Typography carries the brand.** Display faces with real character, body faces chosen for long-form legibility at 17 to 18px. Fonts are self-hosted so there is no third-party request and no layout shift.
- **Depth over decoration.** Layered surfaces, soft multi-stop shadows, inset light, gradient fades between sections instead of hard edges. One accent colour per theme, used sparingly.
- **Contrast that stays clean.** Big type against generous whitespace, dark bands inside light themes and light bands inside dark ones, never more than two surface tones per screen.
- **Imagery of outcomes, not procedures.** People moving, resting, working, laughing. No needles, no vials in hands, no operating rooms.
- **Motion with a purpose.** Scroll-driven reveals, parallax layers and fades that direct attention down the scan path. Nothing loops in the reading area. Reduced-motion switches it all off.
- **Budget.** Under 35 KB gzipped CSS, under 10 KB gzipped JS, Lighthouse 95+ on phones, WCAG AA throughout.

**Candidate typefaces** (all free for commercial use; Google Fonts under the Open Font Licence, Fontshare under the ITF free licence; self-hosted either way)

| Role | Options |
| --- | --- |
| Editorial serif | Fraunces, Instrument Serif, Bodoni Moda, Newsreader, Gambetta, Sentient, Zodiak |
| Display grotesk | Clash Display, Archivo (expanded), Schibsted Grotesk, Inter Tight, General Sans, Cabinet Grotesk |
| Body sans | Satoshi, Instrument Sans, Hanken Grotesk, Albert Sans, Plus Jakarta Sans, Onest, Switzer |
| Mono / labels | Geist Mono, JetBrains Mono, DM Mono |

## 5. The five themes

### 5.1 Atelier: editorial luxury

- **Positioning.** A medical journal designed by a fashion magazine. Taste and authority for people who read long-form.
- **Who.** Patients 45 to 65 with high household income; physician-led partner practices.
- **Look.** Bone `#F5F1EA`, ink `#141210`, graphite `#2B2A28`, hairline `#D9D2C6`, one accent: oxblood `#6B1F2B`. Fraunces (display, optical size 144) with Instrument Sans body and Geist Mono labels. Strict hairline grid, numbered chapters, captions under every image.
- **Layering, shading, fading.** Ghost numerals behind headings, headlines that overlap image edges, duotone (ink and oxblood) photography that resolves to full colour on hover, images that fade into the page at the bottom edge.
- **Hero.** Magazine cover: full-width headline with the photo inset between the lines, "issue" style eyebrow, proof chips as a masthead strip.
- **Motion.** Slow fades, hairlines that draw, curtain reveals on images, nothing faster than 600 ms.
- **Icons.** Thin two-tone, ink with an oxblood back layer.

### 5.2 Obsidian: glass and depth

- **Positioning.** The precision instrument. Future-of-medicine credibility for people who track their numbers.
- **Who.** Executives and athletes 35 to 50; modern longevity clinics and telehealth-forward partners.
- **Look.** Gradient mesh from `#0B0F17` to `#121A2B` with two light sources, violet `#6D5DF6` and ice `#9EE8FF`. Frosted glass panels (6% white, 18px blur, 1px inner highlight). Text `#EEF2FF`. Clash Display with Satoshi body and Geist Mono.
- **Layering, shading, fading.** Stacked glass cards at three depths, volumetric light behind the hero, specular highlights that follow the pointer, section edges that dissolve into the mesh.
- **Hero.** Product-launch composition: centred headline, floating glass stat cards (client-supplied figures only), light sweep across the backdrop.
- **Motion.** Scroll-driven parallax between the three depths, light that tracks scroll, counters.
- **Icons.** Duotone with an ice back layer and a soft violet glow.

### 5.3 Sanctuary: botanical calm

- **Positioning.** Restorative, whole-person medicine. The spa standard applied to clinical care.
- **Who.** Women 40 to 65 and couples; med-spa and wellness-clinic partners.
- **Look.** Sage `#8A9A82`, moss `#4B5B47`, stone `#E9E4DA`, cream `#F7F4EE`, charcoal `#2E2B27`, one accent: clay `#B8785B`. Gambetta (display) with Albert Sans body and Marcellus small caps for eyebrows.
- **Layering, shading, fading.** Arch-shaped image masks, grain gradients, soft layered shadows with inset light, image bottoms that fade into cream, botanical line art behind sections.
- **Hero.** Arch-masked photograph on the right, headline left, proof chips as a soft pill row.
- **Motion.** Gentle floats, a slow "breath" scale on hero imagery, parallax leaves.
- **Icons.** Duotone in moss with a sage back layer inside arch-shaped tiles.

### 5.4 Monolith: Swiss precision

- **Positioning.** Evidence, not hype. The theme for skeptics.
- **Who.** Engineers, physicians, buyers who distrust wellness marketing; physician-practice partners.
- **Look.** Black `#0A0A0A` dominant with white sections for contrast, grey `#EDEDED`, one accent: signal red `#E63312`. Archivo Expanded for headlines, Inter Tight body, Geist Mono. Visible 12-column hairlines, oversized numerals, strictly left-aligned.
- **Layering, shading, fading.** Minimal shading by design: hard 1px rules, flat accent bars, images in strict rectangles with red caption bars. Contrast comes from black bands cutting into white sections. Photography of people keeps it from feeling cold.
- **Hero.** Headline set at the largest size on the site, a single ruler line, three proof chips as a data strip.
- **Motion.** Typographic reveals that rise behind masks, rulers that draw, counters. Hover inverts black and white.
- **Icons.** Solid single-weight glyphs.

### 5.5 Aurora: luminous

- **Positioning.** Optimism and vitality. Longevity made approachable.
- **Who.** Longevity-curious 35 to 55, the NAD+ and telehealth audience; consumer-brand partners.
- **Look.** Pearl `#FBFBFD` with mesh gradients in lavender `#CFC4FF`, mint `#BFF3E4`, peach `#FFD8C2` and sky `#CFE7FF`. Ink `#1A1B2E`, accent iris `#5B4BFF`. Plus Jakarta Sans display with Instrument Serif italic accents and Geist Mono.
- **Layering, shading, fading.** Translucent panels over a drifting mesh, iridescent conic-gradient borders, gloss highlights, tinted multi-layer shadows, sections that blend through gradient fades instead of edges.
- **Hero.** High-key photograph with a soft mesh backdrop, large friendly headline, chips as frosted pills.
- **Motion.** Slow mesh drift, hover gloss, counters, soft orb parallax.
- **Icons.** Duotone with pastel back layers inside rounded tiles.

Balance across the five: two dark (Obsidian, Monolith), three light (Atelier, Sanctuary, Aurora); two serif-led, three sans-led; warm, cool and neutral palettes all represented. None of them resembles a Collection One theme.

## 6. Elite features to build once, shared by all five

- Scroll-driven "journey" section (consultation → clinician review → delivered at home → coordination over time) that pins while the steps change.
- Sticky mobile action bar with the primary action and the phone number.
- Candidacy checker: four questions, non-diagnostic, ends in a consultation booking.
- Trust marquee: credentials, accreditation and sourcing marks (client-supplied artwork).
- Cost and financing block with "programs from" pricing.
- Comparison block: in-home regenerative care vs surgery vs ongoing medication, written as questions to ask, not claims.
- Clinician card with credentials and review-board sign-off line.
- "What happens on the day" visual timeline.
- "Before you book" objection cards.
- Image treatments as reusable utilities: duotone, arch mask, bottom fade, grain.

## 7. Delivery plan

1. Approve this brief (swap any concept you do not want).
2. Foundations: self-hosted fonts, the shared modules above, scroll-driven motion utilities, image treatments. Collection One files stay untouched; the build verifies their hashes.
3. Themes, one at a time, each with its own screenshot sheet across the main pages on desktop and phone.
4. Content pass: the two-second scan audit on every page, in every theme, plus the compliance checklist.
5. Full QA: layout regression, contrast, performance budget, cursor and touch checks.
6. Ship as "Collection Two" in the same design switcher, grouped under its own heading, with Collection One still available for comparison. The locked Collection One build also stays reachable on its own frozen URL.
