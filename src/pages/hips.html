Is this you?

The hip that shrank your world

People come to us with hips like these.

  1. Pain in the groin or the side of the hip after walking, and the walks keep getting shorter.
  2. Putting on socks and shoes means sitting down and planning.
  3. You cannot sleep on that side any more.
  4. You have been told it is arthritis and that a replacement is "eventually".
  5. You want an honest second opinion before you see a surgeon, or before you say yes to one.
What we do

What we can honestly offer a worn hip

The treatment, in plain words

A licensed nurse practitioner comes to your home and injects cells from donated, screened umbilical cord tissue, sometimes with exosomes, into the hip joint. The aim is to support the joint's own repair and calm inflammation. Hips sit deep, so the clinician on your call will say whether an image-guided injection is needed and how that is arranged.

Who it tends to suit

Hips with wear or early to moderate arthritis that still have their shape on imaging. People who have tried strengthening, weight management and anti-inflammatories and want a non-surgical option. Candidacy is a clinical decision made on your video call, with your imaging.

What we will not promise

A cure or a guarantee. Stem cell and exosome treatments are not FDA-approved for hips and are investigational. A hip that has lost its joint space or shape is a surgical hip, and we will tell you that plainly and send you back to your specialist with notes.

Questions

What people with hip trouble ask us

Anything else, ask on the call. Get in touch.

Can a hip be treated at home?{{icon:plus}}
Often, yes. Some hips need an image-guided injection, which is arranged at a partner facility rather than at home. The clinician tells you which applies to you on the call, before anything is booked or paid for.
I already have a replacement date. Is it too late?{{icon:plus}}
No. If the date is elective and your surgeon says waiting is safe, there is a window to try a non-surgical option first. If your surgeon says do not wait, we agree with your surgeon.
How soon would I know if it helped?{{icon:plus}}
Any change tends to be gradual over weeks rather than days, and some people notice little. We set the goals with you first, the walk, the stairs, the sleep, and check against them at each follow-up.
