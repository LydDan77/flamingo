FAQ

NAD+ therapy questions answered

What is NAD+ therapy?{{icon:plus}}
NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a molecule your cells use to make energy and repair DNA. NAD+ therapy is a prescription for extra NAD+, as an injection or a form taken under the tongue. A clinician supervises it and builds the plan around your labs and goals. What it changes for a person is still being studied.
What is the difference between NAD+ injection and sublingual NAD+?{{icon:plus}}
The injection goes under the skin or into a muscle, and is shipped to your door for use at home. Sublingual NAD+ is absorbed under the tongue, with no needles. Your clinician recommends the right form based on your goals and labs.
How long does NAD+ therapy take to work?{{icon:plus}}
It differs from person to person, and some people notice little change. Your clinician checks how you are doing at each follow-up and adjusts or stops the plan. Individual results vary.
Is NAD+ therapy safe?{{icon:plus}}
Every patient is reviewed by a licensed clinician before anything is prescribed. Side effects are possible with any treatment, and your clinician discusses them with you before you decide. Some people notice flushing or warmth for a short time. Your clinician supervises the plan and adjusts the dose. Compounded NAD+ is not FDA-approved. Results vary.
Can I get NAD+ therapy at home?{{icon:plus}}
Yes. Both injection and sublingual options let you start NAD+ therapy from home, supervised by the clinical team. No clinic visit required.
