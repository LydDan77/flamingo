What happens during an in-home regenerative visit

Most people have never had a medical procedure in their living room. Here is the whole sequence, so nothing on the day is a surprise.

Before the visit: the consultation

Everything starts with a video consultation. A licensed clinician reviews your history, your imaging and your goals, and tells you plainly whether you are a candidate. If labs are useful, they are ordered then. If you are not a fit, the process stops there, and you leave with a clearer picture to take back to your specialist.

If you are a candidate, you receive a written plan: the protocol, the product and its source, the full cost, and the follow-up schedule. You decide in your own time. Nobody calls to push.

Preparing your space

You need very little. A clean, well-lit room, a chair or sofa where you can sit or lie comfortably, and a flat surface nearby. The nurse practitioner brings everything else, including sterile supplies and the product in its temperature-controlled container. Pets in another room and a glass of water within reach are the only requests.

On the day

  • Arrival and check-in. The nurse practitioner confirms your identity, your plan and your consent, and checks vitals. This is the moment to raise anything that has changed since the consultation.
  • The procedure. The area is cleaned and prepared. For a joint protocol, the injection itself takes a few minutes. The whole visit usually runs 45 to 60 minutes, most of it preparation and aftercare instructions. {{icon:pen}} Client to confirm visit length
  • Documentation. Lot numbers and the certificate of analysis for the product used are recorded in your chart, and you can ask for a copy.
  • Aftercare. Written instructions, what to expect in the first 48 hours, and who to call, day or night, if something concerns you.

The days after

Mild soreness at the site is common for a day or two. Most people return to light daily activity the same day, with specific guidance on exercise and load from the nurse practitioner. The care team checks in by phone or video at set points over the following weeks, and the same team stays with you throughout.

One honest expectation to set: regenerative therapy is investigational for most uses, change tends to be gradual if it comes at all, and individual results vary. The follow-up schedule exists so that you and your clinician can see what is actually happening and adjust.
