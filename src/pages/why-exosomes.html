Exosomes, the signaling layer that drives repair

Stem cells don't repair tissue directly. They send signals. Exosomes are those signals: nano-sized vesicles carrying the molecular instructions involved in the body's inflammatory and tissue-repair signaling.

The Science

What are exosomes, and why do they matter?

For decades, researchers assumed stem cells repaired tissue by differentiating into new cells. The reality is more elegant: stem cells primarily work by secreting signals that instruct the body's own cells to repair themselves.

Exosomes are the primary vehicle for those signals. They are nano-sized vesicles (30–150 nanometers) that bud off from cell membranes and carry a cargo of microRNA, proteins, and lipids. When they fuse with a recipient cell, they deliver their payload and influence the cell's behavior.

In regenerative medicine, MSC-derived exosomes carry anti-inflammatory signals, pro-regenerative growth factors, and RNA sequences that activate tissue repair pathways. They are, in effect, the active ingredient in stem cell signaling.

FAQ

Exosome therapy questions answered

What are exosomes and how do they work?{{icon:plus}}
Exosomes are nano-sized extracellular vesicles secreted by cells, including mesenchymal stem cells. They carry proteins, lipids, and RNA that act as molecular signals studied for their role in inflammatory and repair pathways in recipient cells.
Are exosomes the same as stem cells?{{icon:plus}}
No. Stem cells are living cells that can differentiate and divide. Exosomes are cell-free vesicles carrying the signaling molecules that stem cells produce. That makes them easier to standardize, store, and administer.
What conditions can exosome therapy address?{{icon:plus}}
Exosome therapy is used alongside stem cell protocols for joint degeneration, soft-tissue injuries, chronic inflammation, and recovery support. It is not a standalone therapy for systemic diseases.
How are exosomes administered?{{icon:plus}}
Exosomes are typically administered via targeted injection to the affected area, often in combination with Wharton's Jelly MSC therapy, by a licensed nurse practitioner in your home.
Is exosome therapy FDA-approved?{{icon:plus}}
Exosome therapy for musculoskeletal conditions is not FDA-approved as a finished drug product. It is administered as part of a physician-supervised regenerative protocol using products sourced from accredited tissue banks.
