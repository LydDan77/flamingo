Is this you?

The injury that stalled

People come to us with injuries like these.

  1. A tendon or ligament that is partly torn, confirmed on imaging, that is not healing the way you were told it would.
  2. A sprain, strain or tennis elbow that has gone on for months.
  3. Heel pain first thing in the morning that physical therapy has only half fixed.
  4. You run, cycle, play pickleball or golf, and you want to get back to it without a surgery first.
  5. You are over forty and healing is slower than it used to be.
What we do

What we can honestly offer a stalled injury

The treatment, in plain words

A licensed nurse practitioner comes to your home and injects cells from donated, screened umbilical cord tissue, sometimes with exosomes, at the injured tendon, ligament or joint. The aim is to support the tissue's own repair. Most people keep working with their physical therapist, and the two plans are coordinated.

Who it tends to suit

Partial tears, tendon and ligament injuries and overuse problems that have plateaued after a proper course of rest and therapy. Active adults with clear goals and realistic timelines. Candidacy is decided by a clinician on your video call, with your imaging.

What we will not promise

A quick fix or a guarantee. Stem cell and exosome treatments are not FDA-approved for soft-tissue injuries and are investigational. A complete rupture, a tendon that has pulled away from the bone, or a joint that is unstable needs a surgeon, and we say so.

Questions

What people with injury trouble ask us

Anything else, ask on the call. Get in touch.

How long until I can train again?{{icon:plus}}
Expect a short period of taking it easy, then a graded return guided by the nurse practitioner and your therapist. The timeline depends on the tissue and on you; we give you a range, not a promise.
Do you work with my physical therapist?{{icon:plus}}
Yes, with your permission. Many of our referrals come from therapists, and the plan works best when they stay involved before, during and after.
What if imaging shows a complete tear?{{icon:plus}}
Then we tell you it is a surgical problem and help you get to the right surgeon. Treating a complete rupture with an injection would waste your money and your time.
