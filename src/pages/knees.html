The knee that changed your plans

People come to us with knees like these.

  1. Stairs are a negotiation, especially coming down.
  2. Getting up from a low chair takes a hand on the armrest and a moment.
  3. You have been told it is wear and tear, or "bone on bone", and to come back when it is bad enough.
  4. Kneeling in the garden or on the floor with grandchildren is off the list.
  5. A surgeon has mentioned a replacement, and you would rather try something first.
What we do

What we can honestly offer a worn knee

The treatment, in plain words

A licensed nurse practitioner comes to your home and injects cells from donated, screened umbilical cord tissue, sometimes with exosomes, into the knee. The idea is to support the knee's own repair and calm inflammation. It is one visit, about an hour, with follow-up calls from the same team.

Who it tends to suit

Knees with wear or early to moderate arthritis that still have their structure. People who have already tried the basics, strengthening, weight, injections, and want a non-surgical option before a replacement. Candidacy is decided by a clinician on a video call, with your imaging if you have it.

What we will not promise

A cure, a new knee, or a guaranteed result. Stem cell and exosome treatments are not FDA-approved for knees and are investigational. Some people notice less pain and easier stairs over weeks; some notice little change. A knee that has lost its structure is better served by a surgeon, and we say so.

Questions

What people with knee trouble ask us

Anything else, ask on the call. Get in touch.

How many visits does a knee usually take?{{icon:plus}}
Most knee plans are a single home visit, with follow-up calls at set points over the following weeks. If a second visit is ever suggested, it is explained and priced before you decide, never assumed.
Can I still have a replacement later?{{icon:plus}}
Yes. Regenerative treatment does not close the door on surgery. If your knee needs a replacement down the line, nothing we have done prevents it, and your surgeon gets our notes with your permission.
What should I bring to the call?{{icon:plus}}
Any X-rays or MRI reports, your medication list, and the name of whoever sent you. If you have nothing, that is fine too; the clinician will tell you what would help.
