Start with what the regulator says

The FDA has published consumer alerts about regenerative medicine products, including stem cells and exosomes. The core message is simple: outside of a small number of approved uses, these products are investigational, some clinics market them with claims that are not supported, and patients have been harmed by products that were contaminated or administered improperly.

A clinic that is honest about this will say so on its own pages. A clinic that avoids the subject is asking you not to look.

Words that should worry you

  • "Cure", "heal", "reverse", "regrow". No regenerative product is approved to do any of these for orthopedic conditions, and the evidence does not support the words.
  • "Guaranteed" or "proven". Results vary by person, product and condition. Guarantees are a sales device, not a clinical statement.
  • "FDA-approved stem cells" for joints. The products used in these protocols are not FDA-approved for orthopedic use. If a clinic says otherwise, ask for the approval number.
  • Countdown timers, "last spots", discounts for deciding today. Medical decisions do not need deadlines.
  • Testimonials that promise outcomes. "No pain after one treatment" is an advertising claim dressed as a story, and health-advertising rules frown on it.

Words that should reassure you

  • "Investigational" and "not FDA-approved". Honest about the evidence.
  • "May support". The careful version of what the research shows.
  • "Not everyone is a candidate". A clinic that turns people away is screening properly.
  • Names and licences. The clinician delivering care and the physician overseeing it, written down.
  • A price in writing, before you decide. With financing explained, not hidden in a pop-up.
  • Source and testing details. Accredited tissue bank, lot testing, a certificate of analysis on request.
Hold this site to the same standard. Our commitments are on the what we will not promise section of the home page, and our candidacy rules are published, not hidden.

About reviews

Reviews on a medical website should come from real patients, be published with their written consent, and never promise a result. Rules on fake and incentivised reviews apply to clinics as they do to any business. Where this site shows a review, it is either a real one with consent or clearly marked as a sample. {{icon:pen}} Client to provide verified reviews
