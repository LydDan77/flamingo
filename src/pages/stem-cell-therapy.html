The Process

How stem cell therapy works

{{icon:clipboard-check}}01

Consultation & Assessment

A licensed practitioner reviews your condition and medical history and determines whether stem cell therapy is appropriate for you.

{{icon:clipboard-list}}02

Therapy Planning

If you are a candidate, you get a written plan: what would be done, by whom, and what it costs. You decide in your own time.

{{icon:dna}}03

Stem Cell Administration

A licensed nurse practitioner comes to your home and gives the cells by a targeted injection into the affected area. The visit takes about an hour.

{{icon:heart-pulse}}04

Recovery & Follow-up

Your team checks in at set points over the following weeks. Any change, if it comes, tends to be gradual, and some people notice little.

Key Benefits

What we do, and what is known

{{icon:heart-pulse}}

What the cells are studied for

Researchers study these cells for the signals they send to nearby tissue, more than for turning into new tissue. The research is still early.

{{icon:leaf}}

What studies show so far

In lab work and early studies, the cells release molecules that may influence inflammation and repair around a joint. Published results vary by condition and by product.

{{icon:shield-check}}

Screened first

Every patient is screened by a licensed clinician first. Side effects are possible with any procedure, and we talk them through before you decide.

{{icon:zap}}

An injection, not an operation

The treatment is a targeted injection, given in your home. There is no operating room and no general anesthesia.

{{icon:activity}}

What nobody can tell you

Whether it will help your joint, how much, or for how long. Some people notice a change over weeks. Some notice little.

{{icon:timer}}

Not FDA-approved

Stem cell treatments like this one are not FDA-approved, and are investigational for most uses. Results vary, and we promise none.

Conditions Addressed

Who a clinician may consider

A clinician may consider this treatment for the joint and soft-tissue problems below. Being on the list does not make you a candidate. That is decided at your consultation, from your history and any imaging you have.

  • Osteoarthritis & joint degeneration
  • Sports injuries (tendons, ligaments, cartilage)
  • Chronic back and neck pain
  • Rotator cuff tears & shoulder injuries
  • Knee pain & cartilage damage
  • Tennis elbow & golfer's elbow
  • Plantar fasciitis
  • Soft tissue injuries

FAQ

Stem cell therapy questions answered

How much does stem cell therapy cost?{{icon:plus}}
Because every program is personalized to your condition, the plan, and the number of areas treated, pricing is determined individually and walked through clearly during your consultation.
What results can I expect?{{icon:plus}}
Results vary by condition and by person, and we promise none. Some people notice less pain and easier movement over weeks. Some notice little change. Your clinician talks through what is realistic for you at your consultation.
What are the benefits of stem cell therapy?{{icon:plus}}
We cannot promise a benefit. People usually come hoping for less pain and easier movement in a joint. Some notice a change over weeks, and some notice little. Your clinician sets expectations for your own case.
Can stem cell therapy help with back pain?{{icon:plus}}
Sometimes a clinician will consider it. It depends on what is causing the pain. Pain that shoots down an arm or a leg, numbness, weakness, or changes in bladder or bowel control need a spine specialist first. A clinician reviews your history and imaging at your consultation, and tells you honestly if you are not a fit.
Is stem cell therapy safe?{{icon:plus}}
Every patient is screened by a licensed clinician first. Side effects are possible with any procedure, and we discuss them with you before you decide. The cells come from accredited tissue banks, and every lot is tested. These treatments are not FDA-approved, and are investigational for most uses. Results vary.
