Nobody should talk you out of a surgery you need. Nobody should talk you into one you can postpone. Here is how a non-surgical option fits into that decision, and how to tell when it does not.

Is this you?

The decision you are weighing

People come to us at moments like these.

  1. A surgeon has offered a replacement or a repair, and said the timing is up to you.
  2. You want to know whether there is a reasonable option to try first.
  3. You are worried about recovery time, or about being out of action for weeks.
  4. You have heard about stem cells and do not know what is real.
  5. You want someone to look at your imaging and tell you the truth, even if the truth is "have the surgery".
What we do

What a second opinion from us looks like

The treatment, in plain words

A licensed clinician reviews your imaging and your history on a video call and tells you one of three things: that a non-surgical option is reasonable to try first, that your joint is a surgical joint, or that more information is needed. If treatment makes sense, a licensed nurse practitioner delivers it at home and a physician oversees the plan.

Who it tends to suit

Elective surgeries where your surgeon has confirmed that waiting is safe, in joints that still have their structure. People who want to try a non-surgical option with their eyes open, and who understand that surgery remains available afterward.

What we will not promise

To talk you out of a surgery you need. If the joint has lost its structure or the tendon has fully separated, surgery is the right answer and we will say so. Stem cell and exosome treatments are not FDA-approved for joints and are investigational. Results vary; we promise none.

What happens

Three steps, and a price in writing between them

{{icon:phone}}01

A video call

About thirty minutes with a licensed clinician. Bring your imaging if you have it. You get an honest yes, no, or "we need more information".

{{icon:clipboard-check}}02

A written plan and price

What would be done, by whom, what it costs, and the follow-up schedule. You decide in your own time. Nobody calls to push.

{{icon:home}}03

Treatment, then follow-ups

A licensed nurse practitioner delivers the plan, a physician oversees it, and the same team checks in with you at set points afterward.

Questions

What people with joint trouble ask us

Anything else, ask on the call. Get in touch.

Will trying this first make surgery harder later?{{icon:plus}}
No. Regenerative treatment does not close the door on surgery, and your surgeon receives our notes with your permission. If you go on to have the operation, nothing we have done gets in the way.
How do I know you will not just say yes to everyone?{{icon:plus}}
Because we publish who we send back to their specialist, we put the price in writing before you decide, and a meaningful share of our consultations end with "see your surgeon". Read what we will not promise on the home page.
Should I tell my surgeon I am looking at this?{{icon:plus}}
Yes. Ask them whether waiting is safe and for how long. A good surgeon and a good regenerative clinician respect each other's role, and your care is better when both know the plan.
