The short tour

How a patient moves through the site

Six stops, in the order a patient meets them. Each button takes you to the real spot and marks it.

  1. They start with where it hurts

    The home page opens with the practice in one sentence, then six plain choices: knees, hips, shoulders, back and neck, after an injury, before surgery.

  2. They read a page written in their words

    Each of those pages begins with "Is this you?": stairs, chairs, sleeping on that side. The science comes last, for the people who want it.

  3. They see the price before they decide

    A price range is on the page, with the promise of an exact quote in writing on the call. Nobody has to phone to find out what it costs.

  4. They read what you will not promise

    The site says plainly that this care is not FDA-approved, that results vary, and that some people are not candidates. This is what makes the rest believable.

  5. They book a video call

    Every "Book" button on the site leads to one booking page. Your calendar sits at the top of it. Next to it: who answers the phone, and what happens on the call.

  6. They land on the thank-you page

    After booking, they see what happens next in three steps, how to change the time, and five things to bring to the call.

What you replace

Your details go where the samples are

Anything stamped Sample on this site is a stand-in. On your own site the same spot carries your real detail.

  • Your practice name and logoTop left of every page, the footer, and the page titles.
  • Your phone number, address and hoursThe header, the footer, the contact page and the booking page.
  • Your cliniciansNames, titles and portraits on the home page and the About page.
  • The person who answers the phoneA first name on the booking page, the thank-you page and the pages for referred patients. Here it is {{site.coordinator.first}}.
  • Your price rangeThe cost sections on the home page, the six "where it hurts" pages and the referral pages. Here it is {{site.pricing.from}} to {{site.pricing.to}}.
  • Your reviews and patient storiesOnly verified reviews, published with written consent, and never a promised result.
  • Your booking calendarThe box at the top of the booking page.
  • A page for each provider who sends you patientsOne private page per referring provider, with their name, portrait and note.
  • Your legal pagesPrivacy, terms and the disclaimer are sample text. Your own counsel reviews and replaces them.
What stays

Six things that do not come off the site

They protect your patients and your licence. They are also the reason a careful reader trusts the rest.

  • The plain statement about approvalStem cell and exosome products are not FDA-approved to treat joints and are investigational for most uses.
  • "Results vary"No page promises an outcome, and no patient story describes one.
  • The section on what you will not promiseIt is on the home page and it stays there.
  • The clinician decides who is a candidateIf someone is not a fit, the site says you will tell them so.
  • The notice required in CaliforniaIt appears on the regenerative pages and on the disclaimer page.
  • The honest wording about the first callThe site never calls the consultation free, and never calls a quote a commitment.
Booking

How booking works

  1. One booking page

    The "Book a Consultation" button at the top right of every page, the buttons inside the pages and the bar at the bottom of a phone screen all lead to the same place.

  2. Your calendar sits at the top

    The practice's GoHighLevel calendar goes in the box at the top of that page. Until it is connected the box says so, and a call-back form underneath does the job.

  3. Then the thank-you page

    A booked call or a sent form both land on the thank-you page. Reminders and no-show messages are sent by GoHighLevel, not by the website.
