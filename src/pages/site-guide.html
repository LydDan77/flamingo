The short tour

How a patient moves through the site

Six stops, in the order a patient meets them. Each button takes you to the real spot and marks it.

  1. They start with where it hurts

    The home page opens with the practice in one sentence, then six plain choices: knees, hips, shoulders, back and neck, after an injury, before surgery.

  2. They read a page written in their words

    Each of those pages begins with "Is this you?": stairs, chairs, sleeping on that side. The science comes last, for the people who want it.

  3. They see the price before they decide

    A price range is on the page, with the promise of an exact quote in writing on the call. Nobody has to phone to find out what it costs.

  4. They read what you will not promise

    The site says plainly that this care is not FDA-approved, that results vary, and that some people are not candidates. This is what makes the rest believable.

  5. They book a video call

    Every "Book" button on the site leads to one booking page. Your calendar sits at the top of it. Next to it: who answers the phone, and what happens on the call.

  6. They land on the thank-you page

    After booking, they see what happens next in three steps, how to change the time, and five things to bring to the call.

Booking

How booking works

  1. One booking page

    The "Book a Consultation" button at the top right of every page, the buttons inside the pages and the bar at the bottom of a phone screen all lead to the same place.

  2. Your calendar sits at the top

    Your own calendar from GoHighLevel goes in the box at the top of that page. On this example the calendar is a sample: nothing is booked. Until a real calendar is connected, a call-back form underneath does the job. Once it is connected, the calendar takes the form's place.

  3. Then the thank-you page

    A booked call or a sent form both land on the thank-you page. Reminders and no-show messages are sent by GoHighLevel, not by the website.
