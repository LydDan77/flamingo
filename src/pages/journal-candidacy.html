Who tends to be considered

  • Adults with pain or reduced mobility from degenerative change or soft-tissue wear in a joint, where the joint still has its structure.
  • People recovering from a sports or overuse injury that has not fully separated a tendon or ligament.
  • People weighing elective surgery whose surgeon has confirmed that waiting is safe.
  • Active adults whose goals are specific: the stairs, the walk, the sleep, the sport.

Who we send back to their specialist

  • Anyone with an active infection, an uncontrolled medical condition, or cancer under active treatment. Speak with your treating physician first.
  • A joint that has lost its structure, or a complete tendon or ligament rupture, where surgery is the appropriate path.
  • Anyone looking for a cure or a guaranteed outcome. We cannot offer either, and we will not pretend to.
  • Anyone who would rather skip labs, follow-ups and an honest conversation about candidacy.
Regenerative therapy is investigational for most musculoskeletal uses and is not FDA-approved to treat any orthopedic condition. Candidacy is a clinical judgement made by a licensed clinician on your consultation, never by this page.

How the decision is made

The consultation is a video call with a licensed clinician. They review your history, your medications, your imaging if you have it, and your goals. They may order labs. They will tell you one of three things: that you are a candidate and here is the written plan, that you are not and here is why, or that they need more information first. All three are useful answers.

What a written plan includes

The protocol and the product, with its source. The clinician delivering it and the physician overseeing it. The full cost and the financing options. The follow-up schedule. The honest expectation that individual results vary. You take it away and decide in your own time.
