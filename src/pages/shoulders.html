Is this you?

The shoulder that wakes you up

People come to us with shoulders like these.

  1. Reaching overhead or behind your back has a catch in it.
  2. You wake when you roll onto that side.
  3. Physical therapy helped, then plateaued.
  4. An MRI shows wear or a partial tear of the rotator cuff, not a complete one.
  5. A surgeon has mentioned repair or a replacement and you want to understand the alternatives first.
What we do

What we can honestly offer a worn shoulder

The treatment, in plain words

A licensed nurse practitioner comes to your home and injects cells from donated, screened umbilical cord tissue, sometimes with exosomes, into the shoulder joint or around the tendon. The aim is to support repair and calm inflammation in tissue that is worn or partly torn. Many people keep working with their physical therapist alongside.

Who it tends to suit

Shoulders with wear, tendon irritation or partial rotator cuff tears that have plateaued with therapy. People who want to avoid or postpone surgery and have realistic goals: sleep, reach, the garden, the swim. Candidacy is decided by a clinician on your video call, with your imaging.

What we will not promise

A cure or a guarantee. Stem cell and exosome treatments are not FDA-approved for shoulders and are investigational. A complete rotator cuff tear or a frozen shoulder is a different problem; both usually need a surgeon or a specialist, and we say so rather than treat them.

Questions

What people with shoulder trouble ask us

Anything else, ask on the call. Get in touch.

Should I stop physical therapy?{{icon:plus}}
Usually not. Most clinicians prefer you keep the strengthening going, with a short pause around the visit that the nurse practitioner explains. If a physical therapist sent you, we keep them in the loop with your permission.
Is a partial tear different from a full tear?{{icon:plus}}
Yes, and it matters. A partial tear still has tissue to work with. A full-thickness tear with the tendon retracted is a surgical problem, and we would be doing you a disservice to say otherwise.
Will I be able to lift my arm afterward?{{icon:plus}}
You will be asked to take it easy for a day or two, then return to light use with specific guidance. Any change in pain or reach, if it comes, tends to be gradual over weeks.
